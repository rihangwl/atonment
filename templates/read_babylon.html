The Richest Man in Babylon

by George S. Clason

The Man Who Desired Gold

Bansir, the chariot builder of Babylon, sat upon the low wall surrounding his home and stared sadly at the unfinished chariot before him.

His strong arms hung idle while his thoughts wandered toward the golden palaces and overflowing treasures owned by the wealthy men of the city.

Babylon was the richest city in the ancient world. Its towering walls, magnificent temples, and bustling markets displayed wealth beyond imagination.

Yet among all that prosperity, Bansir remained poor. Day after day he labored under the blazing sun, but his purse was always empty.

Soon his close friend Kobbi, the musician, approached carrying his lyre. Seeing Bansir troubled, he smiled and said, “May the gods bless you with great wealth, my friend.”

Bansir laughed bitterly. “Wealth? I have none. I work endlessly, yet my family still struggles for bread.”

Kobbi sighed in agreement. “The same curse follows me. I entertain the rich with music, but silver escapes my hands as quickly as water.”

The two friends reflected upon their youth, remembering how they had once dreamed of becoming prosperous men.

“Why,” asked Bansir, “do some men acquire gold so easily, while others who labor harder remain poor forever?”

Kobbi lowered his voice. “Perhaps we were never taught the laws of wealth.”

At that moment they remembered Arkad, their childhood companion who had become the richest man in Babylon.

Arkad’s riches were legendary. Kings sought his counsel, merchants respected his wisdom, and beggars envied his fortune.

Yet Arkad had once been as poor as they were. If anyone understood the secret of gold, surely it was him.

“Let us visit Arkad,” Kobbi proposed. “Perhaps he will reveal how an ordinary man may acquire wealth.”

Bansir immediately agreed. For the first time in many months, hope stirred within him.

Together they walked through the crowded streets of Babylon, passing merchants selling spices, silks, jewels, and perfumes.

Everywhere they looked they saw signs of riches— golden ornaments, fine robes, and luxurious chariots.

Yet they also saw laborers burdened with exhaustion, working endlessly while remaining trapped in poverty.

At last they arrived at the magnificent home of Arkad. Marble pillars towered above them, and servants moved silently through the grand halls.

Arkad welcomed his old friends warmly. Though enormously wealthy, he had not forgotten the days of his humble beginnings.

“Tell me,” said Arkad, “why do your faces carry such sorrow?”

Bansir spoke honestly. “We desire wealth, yet gold continually escapes us. Teach us the wisdom that made you rich.”

Arkad smiled thoughtfully. “Gold comes gladly and in increasing quantity to the man who understands its laws.”

He invited them to sit beside him beneath the warm Babylonian evening sky.

“I was once poor like you,” Arkad began. “But I learned a lesson that changed my destiny forever: a part of all I earn is mine to keep.”

The words sounded simple, yet Bansir and Kobbi listened carefully.

Arkad continued, “Most men pay everyone except themselves. They give coins to merchants, landlords, and craftsmen, but keep nothing for their own future.”

“Gold,” he explained, “is like a servant. If you save it and put it to work wisely, it multiplies and labors for you endlessly.”

The old lamps of Babylon flickered softly as the night deepened. The two friends realized they had spent their lives earning money, but never learning how to keep it.

And so began their journey toward understanding the ancient secrets of wealth, discipline, and financial freedom.
